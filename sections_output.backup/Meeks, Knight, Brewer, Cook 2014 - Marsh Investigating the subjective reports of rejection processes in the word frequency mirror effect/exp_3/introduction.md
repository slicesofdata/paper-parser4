## 7. Experiment 3

In Experiment 3, we wanted to know whether the differences in rejection classifications would still occur when we manipulated WF between-subjects. The WF mirror effect is often found in both mixed and pure lists (e.g., Clark & Burchett,

1994; Dorfman & Glanzer, 1988; Gorman, 1961; McCormack & Swenson, 1972) and the hit rate is due to recollection in both cases (Dewhurst et al., 2004). Dewhurst et al., however, did not find the correct rejection component of the WF mirror effect in a pure list manipulation. Malmberg and Murnane (2002) also found that the false alarm rate for LWF decreased with the presence of more studied HWF items. It is possible, thus, that participants are not sensitive to memorability differences in new items when presented with all LWF or HWF items because relative differences between the memorability for these items is not diagnostic of list membership. If this is the case, there should be no differences in Would Have Remembered responses between LWF and HWF words. As in Experiment 2, CV was still manipulated within-subjects. We chose a between-subjects design to prevent exposure to any items of contrasting WF during the experiment.
